\subsection{Additional results}
\label{app:additional-results}

\Cref{fig:bias_variance_by_n,fig:bias_variance_by_noise} repeat the bootstrap
decomposition of \cref{fig:bias_variance_tradeoff_bootstrap} for other sample
sizes and noise levels (seed 42, $B = 100$, 80/20 split). More data lowers the
variance at every degree, while the bias, a property of the model class, hardly
changes; the minimum therefore moves from $p = 4$ at $n = 50$ to $p = 12$ at
$n = 400$, and for $n=400$ the variance stays below $\sigma^2$ up to $p\approx
16$. More noise scales up the variance and the floor $\sigma^2$ but leaves the
bias against $f$ essentially unchanged, so noisier data favor simpler models.
For $n\leq 100$ the curves beyond the minimum are irregular, for the
extrapolation reason discussed in \cref{subsec:results-selection}.
