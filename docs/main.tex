\begin{abstract}
Runge's function, the classic example of polynomial interpolation diverging
with increasing degree, is a demanding test of how model complexity is
chosen from noisy data. We fitted polynomials of degree up to 20
to 100 noisy samples with ordinary least squares, Ridge and Lasso regression,
estimated the test error with the bootstrap and k-fold cross-validation, and
replaced the closed-form solutions by our own gradient-descent code. Since the data were synthetic, we compared every estimate
with the true error. The bootstrap and cross-validation selected
median degrees of 8 and 10, against 14 for the true optimum, because their
estimates are dominated by extrapolation beyond the resampled training range.
Over 31 data sets, Ridge lowered the cross-validation error of least squares by
a median of 5\% but not the held-out error, and subgradient-descent Lasso did
worse. Momentum and Adam reached the closed-form solution 25
and 9 times faster than plain gradient descent at degree 5, yet no optimizer
converged at degree 10 or higher without regularization. For polynomial models,
how the error is estimated matters more than which penalty is used.
\end{abstract}
